Season Roast

AI-written satire, reviewed for publication

Latest twist: this isn’t a slow-burning league — it’s Patrick M.’s personal demolition derby. Two reported nights, two “wins,” 24 points, and eight knockouts. If this season is a movie, Patrick already has the bully pulpit, the highlight reel, and the credit card receipts from the table.

Season highlights you can’t ignore
- Patrick’s dominance is simple and brutal: he turned up, knocked out almost everybody who mattered (eight KOs across the two nights), and translated that into two flawless tournament wins and a perfect 2-for-2 on the podium. That’s 13 points the first night, 11 the second — the arithmetic of humiliation.
- The points race is still close enough to keep other egos interested: Brad M. sits second in the standings with 19 points, a respectable 5 back of Patrick. Rich is crawling at 18 points after bouncing back for a third-place finish on 11/04; Don and Branson trail just behind with 17 and 12 respectively.
- Patrick’s knockout ledger reads like a hit list. He finished off Greg, Rich and Branson on 11/04 and then went full executioner on 11/11 — Marc D., Rich (again), Don, Brad M., and Rick M. That 2-0 head-to-head knockout count against Rich is the season’s nastiest little rivalry so far.

Table villains, sinners and survivors
- Rich deserves a roast and a medal: most logged rebuys (two), the beneficiary of the add-on-less economy and a man who literally bought his way back into contention. He rebought on both nights and parlayed that into the season’s lone third-place finish on 11/04. He also got KO’d twice by Patrick across the two reports — which must sting like a $15 rebuy.
- Brad M. has been the table’s reliable menace without the trophy case: several early knockouts credited to him (Brad C., Jerry C., Don) and a third-place payout on 11/11. He’s consistent enough to make this interesting but not yet consistent enough to threaten Patrick’s coronation.
- Branson showed up and made noise on 11/04 — second-place finish that night and some tidy knockouts — but Patrick took him out at the end. Branson is the kind of player who looks dangerous until the leaderboard is read.

Comebacks, clutch plays, and cruel mathematics
- The best comeback line belongs to Rich on 11/04: he rebought and finished third, turning a sobering early exit into a payday. It was textbook salvage work even if the sequel the next week wasn’t as kind.
- Don and Greg did the mid-table grind: useful knockouts and steady points that keep them in the mix (Don at 17 points, Greg at 11), but neither has challenged for a win yet in the reported nights.
- The league’s buy-in economy did its thing: 13 entrants and three rebuys on 11/04 created a $195 pool; 11 entrants and one rebuy on 11/11 created $165. No add-ons, so everybody chose to either fold or rebuy early and live with it.

Rivalries worth watching
- Patrick vs. Rich is the obvious one: 2-0 in reported knockout meetings, Rich’s two rebuys can’t buy victory over Patrick’s raw momentum. If Patrick keeps collecting knockouts like stamps, Rich will be left autographing consolation checks.
- Patrick vs. the field is a rivalry in the making. Brad M. sits within striking distance — five points isn’t insurmountable — but Patrick’s KOs are more than a lead, they’re a momentum engine. Stop the knockouts, and you stop Patrick’s scoring train.

Final assessment (cold, honest)
- This is a short sample size, but the numbers tell a clear story: two tournaments, two wins, perfect top-3 conversion for Patrick. The rest of the league has time to adapt, collude, or complain about variance, but right now the only predictable thing is Patrick making the payline and making a mess of the knockout list.
- If you like drama, keep showing up. If you like hope, buy a rebuy like Rich and prepare to fight for every inch. If you’re Patrick, maybe buy a trophy before the other guys figure out how to double-team the knockout king.

Roast grade: Patrick — A for efficiency, the rest of the field — B- for attendance, F for not finding a plan that stops the man with eight knockouts.
